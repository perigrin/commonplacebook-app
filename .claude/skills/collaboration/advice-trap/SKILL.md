# The Advice Trap: Taming Your Advice Monster

An advanced coaching framework from Michael Bungay Stanier that builds on The Coaching Habit. This teaches you **how to actually change your behavior** when you know you should stay curious but your Advice Monster keeps taking over.

**Core Insight**: Your Advice Monster has three distinct personas, each driven by different needs. Until you can identify which one is active and apply specific tactics to tame it, staying curious will remain aspirational rather than actual.

## The Problem: Being in the Advice Trap

**The Advice Trap**: When giving advice is your default management style, even though it doesn't work well.

### Why Your Advice Doesn't Work

1. **You're solving the wrong problem**: The first challenge mentioned is rarely the real challenge
2. **You're proposing mediocre solutions**: You don't have the full picture—just conjecture, bias, and first-idea-itis

### The Real Cost: Failed Leadership

When advice is your dominant mode, damage occurs at four levels:

1. **Demotivates advice-receivers**: Reduces autonomy, mastery, and purpose; creates over-dependence
2. **Overwhelms advice-givers**: You're doing everyone's job, becoming a bottleneck
3. **Compromises team effectiveness**: Teams can't find real challenges or work well together
4. **Limits organizational change**: Generates waste, leaches innovation, reduces agility

## THE THREE ADVICE MONSTER PERSONAS

Your Advice Monster dresses up in three different costumes. Each has different triggers, different drivers, and requires different tactics.

### Tell-It Monster

**Core belief**: "You must have the answer! If you don't Tell-It, nothing will get solved and we'll fail."

**Driver**: Need to prove **competence**, demonstrate expertise, show you're smart

**When it appears**:
- Time feels short and urgent (which is always)
- You're in authority/senior role
- You know the answer
- You want to "add value"
- Your expertise is being questioned

**Field notes**:
- Loves the spotlight
- Dresses in plumage of authority, seniority, wisdom
- Moves in packs (everyone's Tell-It can activate together)
- Wants you to believe you know best

**Typical behaviors**:
- Stop listening as soon as solution appears in your head
- Blurt out ideas as soon as you think of them
- Give the solution you "know" is best for them
- Take lead from start and wrap up at the end

### Save-It Monster

**Core belief**: "You must be responsible for it all! If you don't Save-It and rescue everyone, we'll fail."

**Driver**: **Empathy** and caring; wanting to protect others from pain and struggle

**When it appears**:
- Someone is in distress, frustrated, or upset
- Potential conflict is at play
- Someone is playing Victim role
- You see someone struggling with something hard

**Field notes**:
- Common, but camouflaged as "being helpful"
- Faint odor of burning martyr
- Hits top velocity confronted with Victim
- Wants you to believe you're the most responsible person here

**Typical behaviors**:
- Take on responsibility for solving problems that aren't yours
- Don't ask them what they think the answer is
- Fill awkward silences
- Jump in to mediate and fix relationships

**The paradox**: Saving them robs them of growth. Your rescuing disempowers them.

### Control-It Monster

**Core belief**: "You must stay in control! If you don't Control-It and manage it all, we'll fail."

**Driver**: **Fear of losing control**, anxiety about outcomes, need for certainty

**When it appears**:
- Outcomes feel uncertain
- Someone suggests approach different from yours
- You're not sure where conversation is going
- Things feel like they might go off the rails
- Unknowns or ambiguity emerge

**Field notes**:
- Ever-present but discreet, manipulative, background operator
- Delusions of grandeur
- Noticeable grip strength, can't let go
- Skittish around empowerment
- Wants you to believe you're the only one stopping chaos

**Typical behaviors**:
- Take lead from start and manage the ending
- Get anxious when not sure where conversation is going
- Sort out situation anytime it feels off-rails
- Steer people toward "the right way" (your way)

### The Shared DNA of All Three Personas

No matter which persona, at the core the Advice Monster believes:

**You're better than the other person.**

Not smarter, wiser, more resilient, capable, competent, courageous, original, moral, generous, or trustworthy enough.

**They're not good enough.**

Sit with how uncomfortable, unsustainable, unscalable, exhausting, and disempowering this is.

## THE FOUR STEPS TO TAME YOUR ADVICE MONSTER

You can't banish your Advice Monster—it's part of being human. But you can **tame** it. This is Hard Change (not Easy Change), so "stop it stop it stop it" won't work. You need these four steps.

### Step 1: NAME IT (Who Let the Dogs Out?)

**Purpose**: Identify the trigger—what sets your Advice Monster off and gets it riled up

**The trigger formula**: Specific type of **person** + specific type of **situation**

**Examples**:
- "When my junior colleague asks for direction in a team meeting"
- "When someone I care about is struggling with conflict"
- "When my direct report suggests an approach that isn't how I'd do it"

**Person types**: Senior/junior/same level, smarter/not as smart, know well/don't know, asks for advice/doesn't ask, challenges you/doesn't challenge, related/stranger

**Situation types**: When you know what's going on, when you don't, when they're helpless, when time is short, when ambiguous, when political, when you disagree, when high stakes, when recurring, when they don't get it, when same mistake again, when easier to do yourself

**Action**: Name the specific person and situation that triggers YOUR Advice Monster

### Step 2: CLAIM IT (Confessions)

**Purpose**: Clarify what your specific Advice Monster behavior looks like in that moment

**Action**: Confess to the bad behavior. What does your Tell-It, Save-It, or Control-It actually make you DO?

This feels awkward. That's normal. Be willing to hold your own feet to the flame.

**Keep it real**: Think about the specific person/situation from Step 1.

### Step 3: TAME IT (Prizes & Punishments)

**Purpose**: Articulate both benefits (Prizes) and costs (Punishments) of the behavior

**Why this matters**: You need to see why Present You does this (the Prizes) AND why Future You pays the price (the Punishments)

**The #WinsNotWins**: Every Prize is short-term and brittle. The Punishments reveal the shadow side.

**Tell-It Prizes & Punishments**:
- Prize: "I'm seen to be adding value with my ideas"
- Punishment: *I believe my only way of adding value is having an answer. I crowd out others' ideas.*

- Prize: "I'm the smartest person in the room"
- Punishment: *I don't let anyone else be smarter. I limit my team's potential.*

- Prize: "I keep things moving fast"
- Punishment: *I don't find the real challenge. We solve wrong problems.*

**Save-It Prizes & Punishments**:
- Prize: "I'm best placed to know what's right"
- Punishment: *I won't let anyone else figure this out. I'll always shoulder the burden.*

- Prize: "I'm the most responsible person here"
- Punishment: *I believe no one else is as committed. I prove commitment by being in overwhelm. I don't allow others to take appropriate responsibility.*

- Prize: "I have unlimited capacity to take things on"
- Punishment: *I'm constantly overwhelmed. I must say yes to everything. I don't focus on what's most important.*

**Control-It Prizes & Punishments**:
- Prize: "I hold the reins because I'm the safest pair of hands"
- Punishment: *I believe no one else can control the situation. I carry the burden of controlling everything. No one else learns to drive.*

- Prize: "I keep us away from unknowns"
- Punishment: *I think unknowns are dangerous. I stop us exploring the new and different.*

- Prize: "I protect us against chaos"
- Punishment: *I carry the burden of eternal vigilance. I don't allow others to manage their own lives.*

**Action**: Identify YOUR specific Prizes & Punishments for the behavior you confessed in Step 2

### Step 4: (RE)FRAME IT (Future You FTW!)

**Purpose**: Get clear on WHY it's worth changing—envision Future You benefits

**Why this is critical**: Hard to let go of the trapeze swing unless you can see what you're leaping to. The benefit must feel aspirational emotionally AND intellectually.

**Tell-It Future You benefits**:
- I "add value" by empowering others, not by offering advice
- I don't always have to have the answer—I can rely on others
- I still share wisdom, but deliberately and selectively, not reactively

**Save-It Future You benefits**:
- I don't need to be responsible for everyone's lives—they're adults
- I support people by supporting them in making choices, not making choices for them

**Control-It Future You benefits**:
- I can trade control for empowerment and engagement
- I can teach people to drive rather than doing all the driving
- I can let in the unknown—often a source of competitive advantage and innovation

**Future You Leadership Qualities**:

**Empathy**: Greater sense of what's real for the other person (I–Thou relationships, not I–It)

**Mindfulness**: Greater sense of what's real about the situation (pause between stimulus and response)

**Humility**: Greater sense of what's real about you (you're important AND not that important; your voice isn't the only or best voice)

**Action**: Identify the Future You benefits that matter most to YOU

## EASY CHANGE VS. HARD CHANGE

Understanding this distinction is critical to successfully taming your Advice Monster.

### Easy Change

- Learning your new phone, new route to office, new skill at work
- You don't know, figure it out, practice, master it
- You're pretty good at this

### Hard Change

- New Year's resolutions you keep coming back to
- Same feedback in annual reviews despite trying to improve
- Keep doing "that thing" even though you don't want to
- **This is taming your Advice Monster**

### Present You vs. Future You

**Present You**: Makes the commitment, sets the intention, decides to change

**Future You**: Faces the actual trigger, experiences the pressure, encounters the real moment

**The gap**: Present You commits to coaching more. Future You gets asked a question in a meeting and immediately jumps into advice mode.

**Why this is EXPECTED, not failure**: Hard Change means Present You's commitment isn't enough. Future You needs specific help to comply when triggered.

### The Practice Pattern

Behavior change isn't a straight line up. It's like climbing Everest:
- Make progress
- Acclimatize
- Establish the new norm
- Retreat to gather strength
- Push forward to next camp
- Repeat

Over 40 phases from base camp to summit.

**Your job**: Lots of small experiments and safe steps forward and back. Practice, get feedback, adapt, repeat.

## SEALING THE EXITS: TERA TACTICS TO HELP FUTURE YOU

When triggered, people's brains scan for danger and look for exits. Your job: use TERA tactics to keep them (and yourself) in the conversation.

**The neuroscience**: Brain scans 5 times per second asking "Is it safe here?" based on four drivers.

### T = Tribe: "Are you with me, or against me?"

**Mantra**: Be on their side

**Challenge**: What barriers can I remove between us?

**Say this**:
- Ask questions (collaborating, not "doing to")
- Small encouragements: uh-huh, yes, nice, great, excellent
- Use "we" and "us" where appropriate
- Check in as human being: "What was the high point of the last week?"
- Acknowledge feelings: "That sounds hard/difficult/exciting"

**Do this**:
- Physical signs of agreement (nod, raised eyebrows)
- Appropriate touch with consent (handshake, shoulder)
- Display shared symbols
- Create common enemy / shared goals
- API: Assume Positive Intent

**What gets in the way**: Control-It gets agitated (removing barriers = reducing control, hierarchy, certainty)

### E = Expectation: "Do I know what's about to happen?"

**Mantra**: Show them the future

**Challenge**: How do I shine light on the path, avoid mystery?

**Say this**:
- Use stage directions: "Let me ask you a question..." "I'm just going to interrupt..."
- Use numbers: "We've got 3 things to cover"
- Use time: "Let's spend next 5 minutes finding the real challenge"
- Ask clarifying questions

**Do this**:
- Explain the process as it happens
- Create beginnings/endings, explain transitions
- Create limited choice (not overwhelming)
- Go meta: "I think we're struggling to figure out the real challenge"
- Tell them what's going on for you: "I'm feeling time pressure"

**What gets in the way**: Control-It doesn't like revealing process (opens it to debate, co-creation, visible failure)

### R = Rank: "Are you more or less important than me?"

**Mantra**: Raise them up

**Challenge**: How do I keep them feeling important?

**Say this**:
- Ask coaching questions (their opinion counts, they set agenda)
- "And what else?" (keeps spotlight on them)
- Let them go first: "I've got thoughts, but what are YOUR first thoughts?"
- Lower your rank: "You know this better than me..." "I could be wrong, but..." "I'm not sure if this will be helpful..."

**Do this**:
- Share answers to questions you ask (mutual exchange)
- Remove obvious barriers (table, podium)
- Adjust clothing (take off tie, roll up sleeves)
- Choose physical positioning (same height, kitty-corner, not standing/sitting)

**What gets in the way**: Control-It doesn't like empowering others; Save-It thinks you're shirking responsibility

### A = Autonomy: "Do I have any say in this?"

**Mantra**: Give them the choice

**Challenge**: How will I find moments for them to have choice?

**Say this**:
- Ask coaching questions (they shape the conversation)
- "What do you want?" (most powerful for Autonomy)

**Do this**:
- Understand the big thing you must control, then uncover small choices within it
- Notice when YOU'VE taken ownership and ended up with action items (not them)

**What gets in the way**: All three personas (Tell-It, Save-It, Control-It) are outraged by giving them control

### Using TERA Together

Think of TERA like a mixing board. Balance the elements to raise the overall TERA Quotient. Some tactics may oppose each other (Tribe vs Rank, Expectation vs Autonomy). Your goal: find the right combination to seal the exits and make interactions irresistible.

## THE SIX FOGGY-FIERS: CUTTING THROUGH CONVERSATIONAL FOG

Six patterns that stop you getting clear on what matters. Each blocks you from uncovering the real challenge.

### The Pattern for Beating Any Foggy-fier

1. **NOTICE** the Foggy-fier (can't address it until you see it)
2. **SAY** what you see (make it explicit for both of you)
3. **ASK** the Focus Question (usually "What's the real challenge here for you?" with different emphasis)

### Foggy-fier 1: Twirling (You're Too Early)

**Appearance**: They tell you the first thing on their mind. You pick it up and run with it.

**The trap**: First thing mentioned is rarely the real challenge. You've leapt in too soon.

**Why it's distracting**: The challenge is RIGHT THERE. You have immediate ideas. All three personas (Tell-It, Save-It, Control-It) nudge you forward.

**What to remember**: First thing might be best guess, starting point, half-baked solution, what they think you think is the problem. Rarely the actual challenge.

**How to beat it**:
- Use the Focus Combo: "And what else?" (acknowledges there's more) + "So what's the real challenge here for you?"
- Ask AWE at least 3 times before focusing

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: I ask "What's on your mind?" and they tell me something
- Instead of: Jumping in and solving it
- I will: Stay curious longer with "And what else?" before "So what's the real challenge for you?"

### Foggy-fier 2: Coaching the Ghost

**Appearance**: They talk about THAT person/situation. You ask "And what else?" and spend 40 minutes focused on that other person.

**The trap**: You're coaching the ghost—the other person who's not here. The spotlight needs to stay on the person in front of you.

**Why it's distracting**: You're using best coaching tactics (empathy, curiosity, listening) but on the WRONG thing. You're contributing to dysfunction.

**What to remember**: You can only coach the person you're talking to. Bring conversation back to them and the impact on them.

**How to beat it**:
- Notice which persona pulled you in (often Save-It: "We'll vanquish that person together!")
- Acknowledge what's going on: "This sounds hard/tricky/frustrating"
- Say what you see: "But I'm interested in how this is hard FOR YOU"
- Deploy Focus Question with emphasis: "What's the real challenge here **for you**?"

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: I find myself Coaching the Ghost
- Instead of: Letting conversation roll on gathering irrelevant information
- I will: Stop by naming it—"It's tricky, and I get how they're frustrating. But this is about you, not them"—then ask "What's the real challenge in dealing with [person/situation] **for you**?"

### Foggy-fier 3: Settling

**Appearance**: Not a bad conversation, but in your bones you feel you're both missing the point. There's an important, awkward, tricky issue you're avoiding.

**The trap**: Rather than push, you collude. "This is good enough."

**Why it's distracting**: Settling is comfortable. Avoids challenging conversation. Control-It keeps it safe. By not challenging them, you're not challenging yourself.

**What to remember**: These issues rarely go away. Conspiring in timidity. The courageous act is to not rescue the conversation. When you look at the hard thing directly, it's often more manageable than you thought.

**How to beat it**:
- Notice the conversation is flabby, going through motions
- Remember you don't know for sure—it's a hypothesis. Combine courage with tentativeness.
- Shift to courage pose (sit taller, breathe, shoulders down)
- Say what you see: "I might be dead wrong, but it feels like we're not yet on the real topic. Is that just me, or do you feel it too?"
- If yes: "So, what **is** the real challenge here for you?" (emphasize "is")
- If no: Either agree and carry on, OR test hypothesis: "You might be right, but could [the topic] be what we should look at?"

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: I notice us Settling and avoiding real issue
- Instead of: Colluding and letting conversation continue about something less important
- I will: Check in tentatively—"I might be off track..."—then "is this **actually** the real challenge for you?" If they're still not going there, tentatively offer what I think it is.

### Foggy-fier 4: Popcorning

**Appearance**: Proliferation of challenges. Pop. Pop. PopPop. PopPopPopPopPoppityPop.

**The trap**: Conversation rapidly out of control. Where do you start? Save-It and Control-It having field day.

**Why it's distracting**: Floodgates opened. Three, seven, fifteen problems. How do you sort this chaos?

**What to remember**: Can only solve one problem at a time. Figuring out which is THEIR job, not yours. Your job: help them get better at figuring out the real challenge.

**How to beat it**:
- Notice Advice Monster wanting to reassert control. Wave hello, don't let it out.
- Say what you see: "There's a lot of challenges on the table here!"
- Resist picking where to start. Ask them: "Of all these, which **one** is the real challenge for you?" (emphasize "one")
- Variations: "Where should we start?" "If we could focus on only one, which would have most impact?" "Which one, if solved, might make others go away?"

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: They start Popcorning, throwing lots of challenges
- Instead of: Picking the challenge I think is fastest/easiest/best
- I will: Get them to choose by saying "We can solve only one at a time" then asking "Which feels like the real challenge for you?"

### Foggy-fier 5: Big-Picturing

**Appearance**: Conversation about issues and trends. Interesting, but not personal. No "I" or "me." All generalities, big pictures, "us/them/we." No skin in the game.

**The trap**: Comfortable for both parties. Going through motions, exchanging information, but no insight, challenge, or growth. Pleasant, not irrelevant, safe. Control-It loves this.

**Why it's distracting**: Executive summary feels useful, but insight/engagement/action happen when you involve the person beyond generalized summary.

**What to remember**: Your job is move from generalized to specific, abstract to personal.

**How to beat it**:
- Say what you see: "This is important, but still pretty high-level"
- Use Focus Question with emphasis on last two words: "What's the real challenge here **for you**?"
- Watch for "we/us/them" tells. Point them out: "I hear you talking about 'we' and 'us' and 'them.' I want to know about you. What's the real challenge here **for you**?"

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: They're Big-Picturing, staying high-level, using "we/us/them"
- Instead of: Keeping conversation safe and generalized
- I will: Interrupt by pointing to what's happening—"As interesting as this is, let's get personal"—then ask "What's the real challenge here **for you**?"

### Foggy-fier 6: Yarning

**Appearance**: Epic story. Endless flow of words and information. Extraordinary detail, exemplary context. Inside you're screaming: "When will it end? When will we get to the point?!"

**The trap**: You're nodding, looking interested-and-concerned-but-caring. You prompt with "Tell me more" or "And what else?" Tell-It loves the detail (more info = better answer).

**Why it's distracting**: We love stories. Feels like being good coach (Active Listening!). Most insidiously, it feeds Tell-It: more detail = more confident in answer.

**What to remember**: You can coach successfully knowing little to nothing of specifics. Often person doesn't need the story either—they know it. They're telling you because you seem interested.

**How to beat it**:
- Know that detail often isn't necessary
- Interrupt (politely but firmly)
- Say what you see: "I'm going to stop you there. I think we've got enough context"
- Ask Focus Question: "What's the real challenge here for you?"

**New Habit Formula**:
- When this happens: They're Yarning, providing epic detail
- Instead of: Nodding along and letting it continue
- I will: Interrupt by saying "Let me stop you there—I think we have enough context" then ask "What's the real challenge here for you?"

## WHEN TO GIVE ADVICE VS. WHEN TO COACH

**Critical nuance**: This is NOT "never give advice." It's "stop defaulting to advice when questions would be more effective."

### Give Advice When:

- **Tactical questions**: "Where's the folder?" (Just tell them)
- **Genuine urgency**: No time for exploration
- **Critical information**: You have info they lack
- **Thorough exploration**: They've already explored options
- **Safety/compliance**: At stake
- **The buck stops with you**: Make the call that's required (ensure this is true, not Control-It)

### Coach When:

- Want to build self-sufficiency and autonomy
- There's time for them to figure it out
- Learning is more valuable than immediate solution
- It's a pattern worth addressing, not one-off
- You want to develop the person, not just solve the problem

### How to Give Good Advice (When Appropriate)

**Define it**: Know the right moments (tactical questions, buck-stops-with-you)

**Diminish it**: Tone down assuredness to reduce pressure
- "Here's my best guess..."
- "I may be wrong..."
- "I'm not sure if this will work..."
- "This is just one idea..."
- "This may be completely off-base..."

**Deliver it**: When it's the right moment, be bold
- Label it: "Let me give you my best advice"
- Be fast, clear, bold

**Debrief it**: Check how advice landed
- "Does that give you what you were looking for?"
- "Is that what you wanted?"
- "Does this feel like the right advice?"
- "Does this idea spark new ideas for you?"

### Leadership Styles (Goleman)

Six different leadership styles. Each has time and place. Coaching is one of six (least utilized, clear driver of culture/engagement/results).

Three others connected to advice-giving: Coercive, Pace-Setting, Authoritative (first two have negative effect on culture).

**Advice-giving is part of leadership repertoire. But if you're going to do it, do it well.**

## QUICK REFERENCE: REAL-TIME DIAGNOSIS

### Which Advice Monster Persona Is Active?

**Ask yourself in the moment**:

**Tell-It?**
- Do I feel need to prove competence or expertise?
- Am I thinking "I know the answer"?
- Do I want to be seen as "adding value"?
- Am I focused on being the smartest in the room?

**Save-It?**
- Do I feel need to protect them from pain or struggle?
- Am I taking on responsibility that's not mine?
- Do I feel like the most responsible person here?
- Am I in Rescuer mode from Drama Triangle?

**Control-It?**
- Do I feel anxious about outcomes or uncertainty?
- Am I wanting to steer toward "the right way"?
- Do I feel need to stay in control?
- Am I worried about chaos or unknowns?

**All Three Share**: In this moment, am I believing I'm better than them? That they're not good enough?

### In-the-Moment Taming Process

1. **NOTICE** the urge (mindfulness—pause between stimulus and response)
2. **NAME** the persona ("That's my Tell-It Monster")
3. **CLAIM** the need ("I want to prove I'm smart")
4. **TAME** it (pause, breathe, notice urge without acting)
5. **(RE)FRAME** it ("This is chance for them to figure it out")
6. **ASK** a question (stay curious a little longer)

### Which Foggy-fier Is Active?

- **Twirling**: They mentioned first thing and you're running with it
- **Coaching the Ghost**: Focus is on another person or situation, not them
- **Settling**: Conversation feels flabby, avoiding the real issue
- **Popcorning**: Multiple challenges piling up
- **Big-Picturing**: Generalities, abstractions, "we/us/them," no personal connection
- **Yarning**: Epic story with endless detail

**Response to all**: Notice, Say what you see, Ask Focus Question (with appropriate emphasis)

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. **Not identifying which persona is active**: Can't apply specific tactics if you don't know if it's Tell-It, Save-It, or Control-It
2. **Thinking all advice is bad**: Missing the nuance of when advice IS appropriate
3. **Present You makes commitments, Future You gets no support**: Not using Sealing the Exits or TERA tactics
4. **Not naming the Foggy-fier**: Can't cut through fog you don't see
5. **Coaching the Ghost endlessly**: Spending entire conversation on "them" not the person in front of you
6. **Settling for safe conversations**: Colluding in timidity rather than pushing to real challenge
7. **Picking which Popcorned challenge to solve**: Doing their job of figuring out priorities
8. **Believing the Prizes justify the behavior**: Not seeing the full cost of Punishments
9. **Expecting Easy Change when it's Hard Change**: Beating yourself up for reverting under pressure
10. **Not practicing the Four Steps**: Knowing the framework intellectually but not applying it systematically

## SYSTEMATIC APPLICATION GUIDE

### Before the Conversation (Present You Preparing Future You)

1. **Identify your triggers**: Use Step 1 (Name It) to know which person + situation combinations activate your Advice Monster
2. **Seal the Exits in advance**: Plan TERA tactics for the specific conversation
3. **Pre-commit to specific response**: "When [trigger], instead of [old behavior], I will [new behavior]"
4. **Know your dominant persona**: Is your default Tell-It, Save-It, or Control-It? Anticipate it.

### During the Conversation (Future You in the Moment)

1. **Notice the urge**: Mindfulness—create that tiny pause
2. **Name the persona**: Tell-It? Save-It? Control-It?
3. **Claim the need**: What Prize am I chasing right now?
4. **Tame it**: Pause, breathe, don't act on the urge
5. **Reframe it**: This is their growth opportunity
6. **Notice the Foggy-fier**: Which pattern is blocking clarity?
7. **Say what you see**: Make the Foggy-fier explicit
8. **Ask the Focus Question**: "What's the real challenge here for you?" (with appropriate emphasis)
9. **Use TERA tactics**: Raise the overall TERA Quotient to keep them engaged

### After the Conversation (Learning and Improving)

1. **Reflect on what happened**: Which persona showed up? When?
2. **Celebrate successes**: Notice when it went well (practice deeply)
3. **Analyze failures**: When did you revert? What triggered it?
4. **Update your Sealing the Exits**: What does Future You need next time?
5. **Practice the Four Steps**: Work through them for specific situations
6. **Track Prizes & Punishments**: Are you seeing the real cost?

### Building the Long-Term Habit

**Remember the Everest pattern**: Not straight line up. 40+ phases of progress, acclimatizing, establishing new norm, retreating to gather strength, pushing to next camp.

**Your approach**: Lots of small experiments and safe steps forward and back. Practice, get feedback, adapt, repeat.

**The commitment**: Change the way you lead forever. Become Future You.

## INTEGRATION WITH COACHING HABIT

The Advice Trap builds on The Coaching Habit's seven questions. Use them together:

**From Coaching Habit**:
- "What's on your mind?" (Kickstart Question)
- "And what else?" (AWE Question—best in the world)
- "What's the real challenge here for you?" (Focus Question)
- "What do you want?" (Foundation Question)
- "How can I help?" (Lazy Question)
- "If you're saying Yes to this, what are you saying No to?" (Strategic Question)
- "What was most useful for you?" (Learning Question)

**From Advice Trap**:
- Identify which Advice Monster persona is active (Tell-It, Save-It, Control-It)
- Apply the Four Steps (Name, Claim, Tame, Reframe)
- Notice and name the Foggy-fiers (Twirling, Coaching Ghost, Settling, Popcorning, Big-Picturing, Yarning)
- Use TERA tactics to Seal the Exits (Tribe, Expectation, Rank, Autonomy)
- Understand Easy vs Hard Change (Present You vs Future You)
- Know when advice IS appropriate (Define, Diminish, Deliver, Debrief)

**Together they create**: A complete system for staying curious longer and giving advice more strategically.

**Remember**: The Advice Trap is about taming, not banishing. Your Advice Monster is part of being human and trying to help. But when it believes you're better than the other person and they're not good enough, it disempowers them, overwhelms you, and damages your leadership.

**The shift**: From "I must have the answer/save everyone/control everything" to Future You Leadership grounded in empathy, mindfulness, and humility.

**The practice**: Systematic application of the frameworks—especially in the moment when triggered—transforms aspiration into actual behavior change.
