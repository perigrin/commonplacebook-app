# The Bottleneck Rules

## Overview

Clarke Ching's "The Bottleneck Rules" translates Eli Goldratt's Theory of Constraints (TOC) into an immediately actionable framework for everyday knowledge workers. Where Goldratt's "The Goal" requires hundreds of pages in a factory setting, Ching delivers practical bottleneck management applicable to any workplace in "less time than it takes to hard-boil an egg."

**Central Insight:** The Bottleneck Rules (deliberate double meaning: it's THE rule, and the bottleneck RULES your system). The bottleneck - the resource that cannot keep up with demand - determines your entire system's output. Everything else is secondary.

## Core Definition

### What is a Bottleneck?

A **bottleneck** is a resource - a person, a machine, a computer CPU, a traffic intersection, a slow internet connection, an airport runway - that **cannot keep up with the demand** placed on it.

**Critical Distinction:** When searching for bottlenecks:
- ❌ Don't look for the step that takes the most processing time
- ✅ Look for the longest queue or the lowest capacity relative to demand

**System Impact:** In multi-step processes, the bottleneck determines the speed and output of the entire process. Speeding up the bottleneck speeds up the entire process. Speeding up non-bottleneck resources has no impact on overall system output.

## The Three Foundational Principles

### Principle 1: The Bottleneck Determines a System's Output

The bottleneck is in charge of your system's output. You can either manage it, or let it manage you.

**Examples:**
- Airport runway's self-imposed limit of 6 flights/hour determined entire airport output
- Coffee machine determined how many coffees could be made for entire terminal
- Slow WiFi determined data transfer speed between iPad and Dropbox
- Sinead was only person who could approve invoices (step 4), limiting entire team to 20 invoices/day

**The Rule:** "The Bottleneck Rules" - there is only one bottleneck rule, and the bottleneck rules the system.

### Principle 2: A Simple Recipe to Find and Manage Bottlenecks

This is the **FOCCCUS Formula** (detailed below) - a systematic seven-step approach moving from finding the bottleneck, to optimizing it, to coordinating the system around it, to strategic thinking about where bottlenecks should be.

### Principle 3: The "If Everyone is Busy, We Must Be Productive" Myth

**Common (Wrong) Belief:** Maximize productivity by keeping everyone 100% utilized.

**The Truth:** If your team runs faster than your bottleneck, they're just being busy, not productive.

Creating more work for non-bottleneck resources doesn't increase system output - it just creates more work-in-progress inventory waiting for the bottleneck.

**Key Quotes:**
- "Being busy ≠ being productive"
- "An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage"

**Sinead's Example:** Her team didn't need to work harder. She didn't need to hire more people. All she needed was to find her team's bottleneck, then think.

## The FOCCCUS Formula

Clarke Ching's modernized, accessible version of Goldratt's Five Focusing Steps:

- **F**ind the bottleneck
- **O**ptimise the bottleneck
- **C**oordinate the non-bottlenecks
- **C**ollaborate - help the bottleneck
- **C**urate the bottleneck's work
- **U**pgrade the bottleneck (invest)
- **S**tart again (strategically)

### Mapping to Goldratt's Five Focusing Steps

| Goldratt's 5 Steps | FOCCCUS Formula |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Identify          | Find            |
| Exploit           | Optimise        |
| Subordinate       | Coordinate, Collaborate, Curate |
| Elevate           | Upgrade         |
| Go back to step 1 | Start again (strategically) |

**Why FOCCCUS?** Easier to remember, more concrete words, no negative connotations (vs. "exploit" and "subordinate").

**Spending Philosophy:** While strict TOC doesn't spend money until Elevate, Ching is pragmatic about small investments in earlier steps (like better tools). Hold off on **big money** until Upgrade/Elevate.

## Step 1: FIND the Bottleneck

**Objective:** Identify which resource cannot keep up with demand and is limiting system output.

### Method

1. Map out your process steps
2. For each step, estimate roughly how much work can be processed (capacity)
3. Look for the step with the lowest capacity relative to demand
4. Identify which specific resource within that step is the constraint

### Key Questions

- What are the steps in this process?
- Roughly how much work can be processed at each step?
- Where is the longest queue forming?
- Which resource cannot keep up with the demand placed on it?

### Examples

**Andy Grove's Breakfast (from High Output Management):**
- Step 1 (toast): 90 slices/hour
- Step 2 (coffee): 800 cups/hour
- Step 3 (eggs): 200 eggs/hour
- Demand: 120-150 breakfasts/hour
- **Bottleneck:** The toaster (only 90 slices/hour when demand is 120-150/hour)

**Sinead's Invoice Processing:**
- Step 1 (open mail): 200 invoices/day
- Step 2: 80 invoices/day
- Step 3: 50 invoices/day
- Step 4 (approval): **20 invoices/day** ← THE BOTTLENECK
- Step 5 (automated): 10,000 invoices/day

The bottleneck was Sinead herself - she was the only person authorized to approve invoices (step 4).

### Critical Insight

**Don't confuse "everyone is busy" with "everyone is a bottleneck."**

There is typically only one true bottleneck (or occasionally two) in a system, even if everyone feels overwhelmed.

### On Discovery

**"I see bottlenecks"** (the book's opening theme, like "The Sixth Sense"):
- Once you learn to see bottlenecks, you'll see them everywhere
- Like a good movie plot twist, they're obvious in hindsight
- First time hits you: **"How on earth did I not see that until now?"**
- Something seemingly harmless has been sitting in plain sight, controlling your workplace

**Why hard to see:**
- All the obvious bottlenecks have been found and tackled
- Only devious ones remain
- But there aren't that many places they can hide
- Can still be found with systematic approach

## Step 2: OPTIMISE the Bottleneck

**Objective:** Deliberately focus intensely on the bottleneck's work and find ways to "squeeze more work" out of it without spending significant money.

### Philosophy

**"Thinking costs nothing, is often quicker and more fun, and is more powerful than just chucking money at the problem."**

Small tweaks in the right place often make a surprisingly big difference. Those tweaks are often just sitting there waiting to be found.

### Questions to Ask

- Can we eliminate wasted time at the bottleneck?
- Can we reduce the time each unit spends at the bottleneck?
- Can we fix anything preventing the bottleneck from working at full capacity?
- Can we improve accessibility or ergonomics?
- Can we provide better tools for a small investment?

### Breakfast Kitchen Examples

**Toast for 45 seconds instead of 60 seconds:**
- 33% more capacity for free
- Lifts from 90 to 120 slices/hour

**Fix the toaster if only 3 of 4 slots work:**
- 33% more capacity

**Move toaster to more accessible location:**
- 5% more capacity
- **Don't forget:** that's **5% increase in productivity for the entire kitchen**

**Buy wooden toaster tongs:**
- Faster, more hygienic handling
- Small investment, measurable improvement

### Sinead's Example

**Problem:**
- She spent most time on angry phone calls and recruiting
- Instead of approving invoices (the bottleneck work)
- Classic example: bottleneck managing the person instead of person managing the bottleneck

**Solution:**
- Lock herself in meeting room 2 hours every afternoon
- Focus exclusively on approvals (step 4)
- Prioritize angriest suppliers' invoices first

**Result:**
- Easily doubled daily output from 20 to 40 invoices
- This doubled the entire team's output
- Within 3 days: all outstanding invoices processed
- Within a week: stopped recruiting (didn't need more staff)

### Key Principle

**"An hour lost at the bottleneck is an hour lost for the entire system."**

**"An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage"** - it doesn't improve system output.

This is why bottleneck time is so precious and must be managed carefully.

## Step 3: COORDINATE the Non-Bottlenecks

**Objective:** Adjust the production flow so the entire system runs at the speed of the bottleneck.

### Philosophy

**"Put the bottleneck at the center of your process and stagger the other tasks around it."**

Slow down non-bottleneck resources so they run at the same speed as the bottleneck, or maybe just a little faster.

### Andy Grove's Quote

**"If you didn't adjust your production flow to account for the queue, your 3-minute egg could easily become a 6-minute egg."**

### Methods

**Create coordination mechanisms:**
- Establish a coordinator role (e.g., "Chief Toaster and Coordinator") to keep bottleneck busy and manage work flow
- Create rules to maintain steady flow (e.g., "when you take toast from the toaster, replace it with bread immediately")
- Set work-in-progress (WIP) limits (e.g., "always have 5 eggs cooked, ready and waiting, but no more")
- Adjust staffing schedules to support the bottleneck (e.g., partially toast bread during off-peak hours)

### The Chain Analogy

**How do you make a chain stronger?**
- The strength of a chain is determined by its weakest link
- Strengthen the weakest link → chain gets stronger
- Strengthen other links → chain gets heavier but **not** stronger

**Similarly:** Speeding up non-bottleneck resources makes the system "heavier" (busier, more work-in-progress) but **not** faster.

### Key Insight

This step prevents the accumulation of work-in-progress inventory. Without coordination, work piles up in queues before the bottleneck, creating mess and delays.

---

## Step 4: COLLABORATE (Help the Bottleneck)

**Objective:** Find ways for non-bottleneck resources to actively help the bottleneck without cutting corners.

### Philosophy

**"This step is often the most powerful in knowledge work environments."**

Since non-bottlenecks aren't busy all the time, they have **wiggle room** they can use to pick up other work.

### Opportunities to Discover

- Duplicated work that can be eliminated from the bottleneck
- Easier parts of the bottleneck's work that non-bottlenecks can handle
- Preparatory work that can be done ahead of time
- Alternative resources that can assist (people or equipment)

### Breakfast Kitchen Examples

- Could a griller (broiler) help with some of the toasting?
- Could a cook's blow torch assist? (Silly for breakfast, but illustrates creative thinking)

### Knowledge Work Applications

This step is particularly powerful in office environments where:
- People can learn to do simpler versions of bottleneck tasks
- Documentation or templates can standardize easier work
- Automated tools can handle routine parts of bottleneck work
- Senior people can delegate appropriate work to free up their time

### Key Question

**"What can other team members do to help the bottleneck, even if they're not as fast at that particular task?"**

Remember: They have spare capacity, so using some of it still creates net system improvement. Even if they're slower, they have time to spare.

### Quote

**"Find ways for non-bottleneck resources to actively help the bottleneck."**

## Step 5: CURATE the Bottleneck's Work

**Objective:** Carefully choose what work goes through the bottleneck, similar to how museum curators select what goes on display or magazine editors choose what to publish.

### Philosophy

A resource becomes a bottleneck when it can't keep up with demand. While we mostly focus on increasing capacity, carefully reducing or managing demand on the bottleneck can also work.

**Critical Principle:** "Not all work is created equal. An hour at the bottleneck is an hour for the entire system."

### The Curator Metaphor

Just as museum curators select what deserves display from a vast collection, you must select what deserves the bottleneck's precious time.

### Two Approaches

#### 5a. Demand Management
- Reduce unnecessary demand on the bottleneck
- Eliminate work that doesn't add value
- Question whether all work is equally important
- Say "no" to low-value requests

#### 5b. Offering Alternatives
- Provide alternatives that don't use the bottleneck
- Route appropriate work to non-bottleneck resources
- Create different service levels or offerings

### Examples

**Breakfast Kitchen:**
- Offer freshly baked buns (cooked in batches using the idle oven) as alternative to toast
- Uses non-bottleneck resource (oven) to reduce demand on bottleneck (toaster)

**Airport in Bad Weather:**
- Carefully curate which aircraft use the precious runway
- Delay small aircraft with few passengers still on ground
- Prioritize larger aircraft already in the air

**Restaurant During Peak Hours:**
- Train waiters to subtly favor dishes that don't require head chef's time
- Suggest dish B (prepared earlier, just needs plating) over dish A (requires head chef's 20 minutes)
- At peak times, head chef's time is precious

**Software Testing Team (Eve's example):**
- Curated defects according to how difficult they were to test (testing was their bottleneck)
- Talked to audit department to put workarounds in place for most difficult ones
- Focused precious testing capacity on high-value, high-impact defects

### Critical Note

**"This step is often the most powerful but also the most neglected."**

## Step 6: UPGRADE the Bottleneck

**Objective:** If previous steps haven't provided enough capacity, invest money to add more bottleneck capacity.

### Philosophy

**Only after you've thought deeply and optimized thoroughly should you spend significant money.** This is where you "pull out your wallet."

### Methods

- Buy additional bottleneck resources (a second toaster)
- Replace the bottleneck with a bigger, faster version
- Add staff who can perform the bottleneck work
- Invest in technology that increases bottleneck capacity

### Breakfast Kitchen Example

After all other improvements, if still can't keep up:
- Replace with a bigger, faster toaster
- Or buy an additional toaster

### Sinead's Counter-Example

**Initial thought:** She needed to hire more staff

**After finding and optimizing bottleneck:**
- Hiring wasn't necessary
- Stopped recruiting efforts entirely
- Saved significant money and time

### Hospital Example (from Chapter 13)

**Big investment approach:**
- Built and equipped a new operating theatre (significant investment)
- Employed more staff (increased costs)
- Result: Throughput tripled, "annihilated the waiting list"
- Sometimes the investment IS justified, but only after exhausting earlier steps

### Key Timing

This step comes late in the process deliberately. Many organizations rush here first ("we need more people/equipment") when the real solution is better management of existing resources.

### Investment Principle

**Small investments** in earlier steps (like toaster tongs or better tools) can be pragmatic.

**Hold off on large capital expenditures** until you've exhausted thinking-based improvements.

## Step 7: START AGAIN (Strategically)

**Objective:** Recognize that bottlenecks move, and think strategically about where the bottleneck should be.

### Philosophy

Once you've improved or eliminated a bottleneck, a different resource will become the new bottleneck. Be proactive about where you want it to move to.

### Two Scenarios

#### 7a. The Bottleneck Will Move (Be Ready)

After increasing capacity at the current bottleneck, one of two things will happen:
1. The system can now handle all demand (no bottleneck in the system)
2. A different resource becomes the new bottleneck

**Breakfast Kitchen Example:**
- As toasting capacity increases, egg boiling will likely become the next bottleneck
- Prepare by thinking ahead about egg-boiling capacity

#### 7b. Choose Where the Bottleneck Should Be (Strategic Thinking)

**The Hotel Example:**

A full-service hotel won't last long if it can't feed its guests breakfast. Rather than just follow the bottleneck wherever it moves, think strategically:

**Question:** Where SHOULD the bottleneck be?

**Answer:** The hotel's bedrooms should be the bottleneck, not the toaster, egg cooker, waiter, lift, parking space, or water boiler.

All other resources should have enough capacity to serve the guests staying in those rooms.

**Sinead's Example:**
**"It made no sense for one person in an accounting team to be a bottleneck,"** especially when it jeopardized a large company's enormously expensive strategic project.

**Airport Safety Example:**
- It DOES make sense to deliberately create a bottleneck (reduce runway capacity) when passenger safety is at risk
- It would NOT make sense if the airport had to limit flights because of bathroom or parking shortages

### Strategic Questions to Ask

- Where is the most strategic place for our bottleneck to be?
- Are we bottlenecked on something that truly matters to our business?
- Should we deliberately create a bottleneck somewhere specific?
- What would happen if we chose where the bottleneck goes next?

## The Five Types of Bottlenecks

Clarke Ching identifies five distinct types, each with different characteristics and management approaches:

### 1. Wild Bottlenecks

**Definition:** Often hidden and either unmanaged or poorly managed.

**Characteristics:**
- Not visible to the organization
- Secretly controlling system output
- Causing problems that seem mysterious or unsolvable
- Often discovered through careful analysis rather than observation

**Management Approach:**
- Use FOCCCUS formula to find and tame them
- Once found, they seem "obvious in hindsight"
- Like finding a hidden plot twist in a movie

**Examples:**
- Sinead's invoice approval bottleneck (she was the only approver)
- Many organizations operate permanently at "bad weather" speed due to wild bottlenecks they don't realize they have

**Key Quote:** **"If you can see it, you can manage it. If you can't see it, it's managing you."**

### 2. Tamed Bottlenecks

**Definition:** Don't have as much capacity as we'd like, but they are visible and managed.

**Characteristics:**
- Known and understood by the organization
- Actively monitored and managed
- May still limit system capacity but in a controlled way
- Result of successfully finding and managing a wild bottleneck

**Management Approach:**
- Continue to monitor and optimize
- Maintain coordination around the bottleneck
- Periodically review if it's working on the right stuff

**Analogy:** Like horse wranglers taming wild horses - sometimes the bottleneck disappears after taming (Sinead, Derek), other times it hangs around but is now under control.

### 3. Deliberate Bottlenecks (Throttlenecks)

**Definition:** Designed to deliberately limit flow through a system.

**Purpose:** Control, safety, quality, or strategic advantage.

**Alternative Term:** "Throttlenecks" - the mechanism by which fluid flow is managed by constriction or obstruction (like a car's accelerator, jet engine's thrust lever, steam engine's regulator, or bath tap).

**Examples:**

**Safety:**
- Wellington airport reducing runway capacity to 6 landings/hour in bad weather
- Air Traffic Control deliberately creating bottleneck for passenger safety

**Flow Control:**
- Egg timer with its narrow neck that controls sand flow to measure time
- Wine bottle necks that control pour rate (glug vs. drip)
- Stadium entry/exit points that control crowd flow
- Bath taps (throttles) that control water flow

**Business Strategy:**
- Diamond industry deliberately choking supply to create artificial scarcity and keep prices high

**Process Management:**
- Placing deliberate bottleneck at the start of a process
- Throttling flow of work into the system
- Running entire system at speed of the bottleneck
- Prevents workplace from filling up with half-done work queuing for the bottleneck

**Philosophy:** **"Not too little, not too much, just right"** (Goldilocks principle).

### 4. Right-Stuff Bottlenecks

**Definition:** Tamed bottlenecks whose work has been properly curated so they're working on the right stuff.

**Key Question:** Is the bottleneck being effective? Are you working on the right stuff?

**Importance:** There's no point going on a holiday in your super-efficient, racy new car if you head off in the wrong direction at 100 miles per hour.

**Examples:**
- Airport prioritizing large aircraft over small aircraft when runway capacity is limited
- Restaurant steering customers toward dishes that don't require head chef during peak times
- Eve's team curating defects by testing difficulty and implementing workarounds for hardest ones

**Critical Insight:** The Curate step in FOCCCUS is often the most powerful but also the most neglected. It ensures you're pushing the right work through the team given where their bottleneck is.

### 5. Right-Place Bottlenecks

**Definition:** Not only tamed, but positioned where they're supposed to be strategically.

**Key Question:** Is the bottleneck in the right place?

**Importance:** Not all bottlenecks are created equal. A team may go half as fast if their bottleneck is at resource X compared to resource Y.

**Examples of WRONG-Place Bottlenecks:**
- Hotel toaster limiting the number of guests the hotel can serve (should be bedrooms)
- One person in an accounting team jeopardizing a large strategic project (Sinead)
- Airport bathroom or parking shortage limiting daily flights (should be runway or gates)

**Examples of RIGHT-Place Bottlenecks:**
- Hotel bedrooms as the bottleneck (all other resources sized to support guests)
- Airport runway in bad weather (safety-justified deliberate bottleneck)
- Hospital operating theatre capacity (saves lives, serves the mission)

**Strategic Thinking:** During the "Start Again (strategically)" step, think about whether the bottleneck is in the right place and what you can do to move it to a more strategic location.

## The Vicious Circle (and How to Break It)

### When Bottlenecks Are Unmanaged

**The Downward Spiral:**
1. System can't keep up with demand
2. Customers/suppliers get angry and call
3. These calls take time away from productive work
4. This makes the bottleneck worse
5. Leading to more delays and more calls
6. Staff become stressed and leave
7. Making the problem even worse

**Quote:** "No matter how hard we all work, things keep getting worse. It's a vicious circle."

### Root Cause

**Wild Bottleneck** (unmanaged, managing them instead of being managed)

This is exactly Sinead's situation from the book.

### Breaking the Circle

**Solution:** NOT massive investment

**Actual solution:** Find and manage the bottleneck (FOCCCUS)

**Once bottleneck properly managed:**
- Output increases immediately
- Backlogs clear quickly
- Angry calls stop
- Stress decreases
- Recruitment needs evaporate
- Budget may actually decrease while output increases

### Sinead's Results

**Time to fix:** "Less time than it takes to hard-boil an egg" (3 days)

**Specific result:** Doubled output from 20 to 40 invoices/day by managing bottleneck, not spending money

**Long-term impact:**
- All outstanding invoices processed within 3 days
- Contractors continued working on fiber-optic project
- Stopped recruiting within a week
- Company successfully sold at good price

**The Hindsight Quote:** "How on earth did I not see that until now?"

Bottlenecks are obvious in hindsight. The key is finding them systematically before they manage you.

## Common Mistakes and Misconceptions

### Mistake 1: "Everyone is a bottleneck because everyone is busy"

**Reality:** Busy ≠ bottleneck. There's typically only one or two actual bottlenecks. Everyone else being busy just means there's a lot of work-in-progress inventory.

### Mistake 2: Rushing to hire more people or buy more equipment

**Reality:** Often the bottleneck can be managed with better organization, optimization, and coordination. Investment should come after thinking.

### Mistake 3: Optimizing the wrong resources

**Reality:** Making non-bottleneck resources faster doesn't improve system output. It just makes the system busier and creates more work-in-progress.

### Mistake 4: Stopping at Find and Optimize

**Reality:** The three C's (Coordinate, Collaborate, Curate) often provide the most powerful improvements, especially in knowledge work.

### Mistake 5: Not thinking strategically about where bottlenecks should be

**Reality:** Just following the bottleneck around isn't strategic. Think about where it should be for your business model and mission.

### Mistake 6: Treating all bottlenecks as problems

**Reality:** Some bottlenecks should be deliberate (safety, quality, strategy). The goal isn't to eliminate all bottlenecks, but to have them in the right place and properly managed.

### Mistake 7: Using complex analysis before simple questions

**Reality:** Often just asking "What's your process?" and "How much can each step handle?" reveals the bottleneck within minutes.

## Step-by-Step Application Guide

### For Your First Bottleneck Hunt

**1. Map Your Process**
- Identify all steps in your workflow
- List them in sequence
- Keep it simple - don't overcomplicate

**2. Estimate Capacity (Roughly)**
- For each step, estimate how much work can be processed
- Don't aim for perfection - rough numbers are fine
- Ask: "Roughly how many X can we process at this step?"

**3. Find the Constraint**
- Look for the lowest number
- Look for the longest queues
- That's most likely your bottleneck
- Verify: Is demand higher than this capacity?

**4. Identify the Specific Resource**
- Is it a person, machine, or process?
- Who or what specifically is the constraint?
- Can you name it?

**5. Ask the Key Question**

**"Do you have a bottleneck?"** (Not "Do you have bottlenecks?" - there's usually just one or two)

### When You've Found Your Bottleneck

**Quick Wins (Optimize):**
- What's preventing it from running at full capacity?
- What time is wasted?
- What simple improvements cost little or nothing?
- Can you fix, move, or improve something today?

**System Coordination:**
- How can we adjust everything else to run at the bottleneck's speed?
- What rules or roles would help?
- How do we prevent work piling up?

**Get Help (Collaborate):**
- What can others do to help?
- What work can be delegated or distributed?
- What preparatory work can be done in advance?

**Choose Wisely (Curate):**
- Is all this work equally valuable?
- What should we say no to?
- What alternatives can we offer?
- How do we prioritize?

**Then Consider Investment:**
- Have we exhausted thinking-based improvements?
- Is investment truly necessary?
- What's the ROI?
- Will this create a new bottleneck elsewhere?

**Finally, Think Strategically:**
- Where will the bottleneck move next?
- Where should it be?
- Are we ready?
- Does this align with our mission?

## Why This Framework Matters

### The Accessibility Achievement

Clarke Ching has successfully translated complex Theory of Constraints principles into an immediately actionable framework for everyday workers. What took Goldratt hundreds of pages in a factory setting, Ching delivers in short, practical chapters applicable to any workplace.

### The Core Value Proposition

1. **Speed:** Find and fix bottlenecks in "less time than it takes to hard-boil an egg"
2. **No Investment Required:** Most improvements come from thinking, not spending
3. **Universal Application:** Works in any industry, any process, any team size
4. **Immediate Results:** Sinead doubled output in 3 days
5. **Compound Benefits:** Increased output, reduced costs, reduced stress, improved retention

### The Professional Impact

Understanding bottlenecks provides:
- **Diagnostic capability:** Quickly identify root causes of capacity problems
- **Leadership tool:** Guide teams to focus on what matters
- **Communication framework:** Explain complex situations simply
- **Strategic thinking:** Decide where constraints should be, not just follow them
- **Career advantage:** Solve problems others can't see

### The Broader Insight

"The Bottleneck Rules" reminds us that:
- Complex problems often have simple solutions
- What's obvious in hindsight was invisible before
- Thinking is often more powerful than spending
- System-level thinking beats local optimization
- Strategic placement of constraints matters as much as managing them

## Practical Application Checklist

When facing capacity or throughput problems, systematically apply:

### FIND Phase
- [ ] Map process steps
- [ ] Estimate capacity for each step
- [ ] Identify lowest capacity relative to demand
- [ ] Name the specific bottleneck resource
- [ ] Verify with "Do you have a bottleneck?" (singular)

### OPTIMISE Phase
- [ ] List wasted time at bottleneck
- [ ] Identify barriers to full capacity operation
- [ ] Find quick wins (accessibility, tools, ergonomics)
- [ ] Protect bottleneck time from interruptions
- [ ] Remember: An hour lost at bottleneck = hour lost for entire system

### COORDINATE Phase
- [ ] Establish coordination mechanism or role
- [ ] Set work-in-progress (WIP) limits
- [ ] Create rules for steady flow
- [ ] Slow down non-bottlenecks to match bottleneck speed
- [ ] Prevent queue buildup before bottleneck

### COLLABORATE Phase
- [ ] Identify what non-bottlenecks can do to help
- [ ] Find simpler bottleneck work others can learn
- [ ] Create preparatory work that can be done in advance
- [ ] Cross-train where appropriate
- [ ] Remember: Non-bottlenecks have spare capacity to use

### CURATE Phase
- [ ] Categorize work by value/importance
- [ ] Prioritize high-value work through bottleneck
- [ ] Identify work that can be deferred or rejected
- [ ] Find alternatives that bypass bottleneck
- [ ] Remember: Not all work is created equal

### UPGRADE Phase
- [ ] Confirm all thinking-based improvements exhausted
- [ ] Calculate ROI for investment
- [ ] Consider: additional resources, better tools, more staff
- [ ] Only spend big money after exhausting earlier steps

### START AGAIN Phase
- [ ] Identify where bottleneck will move next
- [ ] Ask: Where SHOULD bottleneck be strategically?
- [ ] Prepare for next constraint
- [ ] Align bottleneck position with business mission
- [ ] Return to FIND with new bottleneck

## Connection to Goldratt's Work

### The Goal (1984)

**Setting:** Factory about to be closed despite huge backlog of customer orders

**Structure:**
1. First half: Managers believe keeping everyone busy maximizes output
2. Middle: Hero realizes output limited by two slowest machines (bottlenecks)
3. Second half: Make those machines run faster → factory runs faster, revenue shoots up
4. End: Develop simple process for finding and managing bottlenecks

**Key Difference:** "The Goal" includes complex accounting and measurement system changes needed for large-scale manufacturing. Most readers will never encounter these situations.

### Why "The Bottleneck Rules" Exists

**The Problem:**
- "The Goal" and other TOC books are too advanced for everyday situations
- Aimed at managers of big businesses solving big problems
- Bottlenecks don't appear until page 203 of Ching's own "Rolling Rocks Downhill"
- First half of "The Goal" is largely about accounting and factory measurement systems

**The Solution:**
"I wrote this book to help everyday, ordinary people like Sinead find and then manage their bottleneck quickly, no matter what industry they happen to work in."

**Ching's Approach:**
- Plucked the three most important lessons from "The Goal" and TOC
- Modernized them
- Adapted them to be easier to understand
- Made them immediately applicable
- Focused on knowledge work environments, not factories

### Further Reading

**Primary Sources:**
- "The Goal" by Eliyahu M. Goldratt (1984) - Original TOC business novel
- "High Output Management" by Andy Grove (1983) - Contains breakfast example and limiting step concept
- "Rolling Rocks Downhill" by Clarke Ching - TOC applied to large-scale software development

**Other TOC Works by Goldratt:**
- "It's Not Luck" - Sequel to The Goal
- "Critical Chain" - TOC for project management
- "Theory of Constraints" - Academic treatment

**Key Concepts to Explore Further:**
- The Five Focusing Steps (Goldratt's original framework)

## Summary

The Bottleneck Rules provides an immediately actionable framework for finding and managing constraints in any workplace:

1. **The bottleneck determines your system's output** - manage it or let it manage you
2. **Use FOCCCUS systematically** - Find, Optimise, Coordinate, Collaborate, Curate, Upgrade, Start again
3. **Being busy ≠ being productive** - if your team runs faster than your bottleneck, they're just creating inventory

**Key Actions:**
- Find the resource that cannot keep up with demand (look for longest queue)
- Squeeze more work from it through thinking, not spending
- Coordinate entire system to run at bottleneck's speed
- Help bottleneck using non-bottleneck spare capacity
- Curate what goes through bottleneck (not all work is equal)
- Only then invest money if needed
- Think strategically about where bottleneck should be

**Remember:**
- An hour lost at bottleneck = hour lost for entire system
- An hour saved at non-bottleneck = mirage
- Thinking costs nothing and is often more powerful than spending
- Small tweaks in right place make surprisingly big difference
- Complex problems often have simple solutions

**The Promise:** Find and fix bottlenecks in "less time than it takes to hard-boil an egg" - without spending money, by thinking clearly about your constraint.
